Executive summary

Fact verified in a cited source · Estimate my calculation from cited figures · Assumption stated input I chose · Hypothesis a claim to test

  • Estimate Broad "workflow automation" is roughly a USD 25–30 billion market in 2026. Four research firms put it between $24.7B and $29.9B, and forecast growth of about 9–17% a year.1,3,4
  • Fact Published market sizes vary by more than 4× depending on definition, from $6.6B for "automation and optimisation software" to $29.9B.4,6 Any figure quoted to a client must name its definition.
  • Fact The five platforms bill on different units: tasks (Zapier), credits (Make), executions (n8n), users or bots (Power Automate) and active flows or credits (Activepieces). The same workflow can cost very different amounts.7,8,11,13,15
  • Fact Pricing is volatile. Make changed its paid-plan structure twice in 2026, and Zapier now counts AI steps as tasks.7,10
  • Hypothesis The best opening for a small service provider is platform-neutral implementation: an automation audit, a cost model and documented setup. Adoption is high, but SMBs report needing implementation help.17,19

Scope and method

Simulated client question: "We support 10–20 SMB clients. Which automation platform should we default to, and what services can we sell around it?"

  • Vendors: Zapier, Make, n8n, Microsoft Power Automate and Activepieces, chosen to cover hosted no-code tools, open-source or self-hostable tools and a suite-bundled tool.
  • Source priority: (1) vendor pricing pages, (2) research-firm report listings, (3) dated third-party pricing trackers, used only where the vendor page doesn't show prices, with lower confidence.
  • Rules: every figure carries a source and date. Where sources conflict, I show the conflict instead of picking one silently. Paid reports were not purchased, so market sizes come from public summaries and listings.

Industry trends

  1. AI steps are being metered like any other action. Fact On Zapier, successful AI steps and tool calls consume tasks, while Zapier Agents run on a separate activity allowance (400 a month on Free, 1,500 on Pro).7
  2. Per-run pricing is challenging per-step pricing. Fact n8n bills per workflow execution, and every plan includes unlimited users and workflows. Its Starter plan includes AI agents and MCP.11
  3. Pricing models are unstable. Fact Make split into Core, Pro and Teams in June 2026, then folded back into a single paid plan in August, according to a pricing tracker. Its own page now bills in credits.8,10 Activepieces moved its cloud product to per-active-flow pricing in 2026.15
  4. SMB AI adoption is broad but shallow. Fact Thryv's April 2026 survey of 561 US SMB decision-makers found 66% use AI, up from 55% a year earlier.17 Upwork reports that 34% of SMB leaders are actively piloting AI agents for workflow automation.18
  5. Implementation is the bottleneck. Fact In Goldman Sachs' 10,000 Small Businesses Voices survey, 73% said more training and implementation resources would help them adopt AI.19
  6. Suite bundling. Fact Microsoft 365 plans include Power Automate with standard connectors. Premium connectors need Power Automate Premium at $15 per user per month on annual billing.13

SWOT analysis: three leading platforms

Strengths and weaknesses are based on cited pricing facts. Opportunities and threats are hypotheses.

Zapier

Strengths
Largest integration catalogue. A free tier, and simple task-based billing.
Weaknesses
Every action step and AI step uses tasks, so multi-step flows get expensive. The entry Pro tier has only 750 tasks.
Opportunities
Agents and AI fields can upsell existing users.
Threats
Per-run competitors look cheaper as workflows grow.

Make

Strengths
A visual scenario builder, a free tier with 1,000 credits and a low entry price.
Weaknesses
Plan changes in 2026 make costs hard to forecast for clients.
Opportunities
Price-sensitive SMBs moving off per-task tools.
Threats
Buyer distrust if pricing keeps changing.

n8n

Strengths
Billing per execution regardless of steps. Unlimited users and workflows, and self-hosting.
Weaknesses
No permanent free cloud plan. Self-hosting needs technical upkeep.
Opportunities
AI-agent workflows with many steps, where per-run pricing is an advantage.
Threats
Non-technical SMBs may find it too complex without help.

Strategic recommendations

  1. Lead with a platform-neutral "automation audit". Map current workflows, count steps and runs, and model cost on two or three platforms. Test: offer it to 10 existing clients and aim for at least 2 paid audits in 30 days.
  2. Default stack by segment. Use Zapier or Make for non-technical SMBs, n8n for technical or high-step workflows, and Power Automate for Microsoft 365 organisations. Avoid one-size-fits-all.
  3. Keep a monthly pricing log. Given the 2026 changes, re-check each vendor page monthly and alert clients to anything that affects their bill.
  4. Sell documentation, not just builds. SOPs and run-books reduce client risk when staff change, and they differentiate from freelancers who only build.
  5. Gather primary data. Run 10–15 SMB interviews to validate the segments and willingness to pay before setting prices.
