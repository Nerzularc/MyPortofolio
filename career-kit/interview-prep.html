Interview Preparation: Questions & Answers

View Markdown sourceBack to portfolio

Answers are written in plain English. Practise them out loud, then make them your own. Use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and be honest that your projects are personal or simulated.

A. General and remote-work questions

1. Tell me about yourself.

I'm a first-year Digital Innovation student at ITS in Surabaya. Outside class I founded BrightLab, a learning community for students preparing for the TKA, which has reached about 700–800 students, and I was secretary of a regional student forum. I'm building a remote career in research and operations support. I like turning messy information into structured, checked outputs, like a scored lead list or a cited research brief. I've documented five practice projects in my portfolio, covering AI response evaluation, lead generation, market research, VA operations and social media operations. I'm looking for a part-time role, 2–4 hours a day, where accuracy and clear communication matter.

2. You don't have paid client experience. Why should we hire you?

That's fair. I don't have paid client work yet, but I have real responsibility: I run BrightLab and coordinate its app team, I handled administration and documentation as a forum secretary, and for MitraCek I ran 5 interviews and 10 field observations. I've also documented my methods in a portfolio, so you can judge my work directly. I'm also happy to do a small paid test task. What I offer is careful work, clear written updates and a habit of documenting everything so it can be repeated.

3. How do you manage working across time zones?

I write both times in every calendar invite, I confirm offsets around daylight-saving changes, and I send an end-of-day note so nobody waits for me to be online. In UTC+7 my afternoon overlaps with UK mornings, and my evening covers US East mornings.

4. How do you handle 2–4 hours a day with other commitments?

I block fixed hours, track time per task and agree priorities each week. If something won't fit, I say so early and propose what to drop or move.

5. How do you communicate when you're stuck?

I try for a set time, usually 20–30 minutes. Then I send one message with what I tried, what I think the options are and what I need, so it can be answered in one reply.

B. AI evaluator questions

6. How do you decide which of two AI responses is better?

First I screen for critical problems: safety issues or fabricated facts and citations. Then I check correctness, then hard instructions like format and length, then completeness and relevance. A fluent answer with a fabricated citation always loses. I write a short rationale that points to the deciding evidence.

7. What is a hallucination? Give an example.

It's content the model presents as true that is invented or unsupported. In my project, one response invented a full citation for a "2019 Stanford study". The better answer corrected the premise and pointed to the real 2015 study by Bloom and colleagues.

8. What do you do if the guidelines don't cover a case?

I choose the most reasonable reading, note my assumption in the rationale and flag the case to the project lead. In my project, "mixed" sentiment wasn't covered by a three-label scheme, so I flagged it as a guideline gap.

9. How do you keep your ratings consistent over hundreds of tasks?

I use anchored rubric descriptions, re-read the guidelines at the start of each session, keep notes on edge-case decisions and spot-check my earlier ratings. In a team I'd join calibration rounds.

10. A response is helpful but breaks a length limit. How do you rate it?

It loses points on instruction adherence and usually loses the comparison to a correct, compliant answer, because the user asked for that constraint.

C. Research and lead generation questions

11. Walk me through how you build a lead list.

Agree the ICP and job titles, build the company list from LinkedIn filters, directories and search operators, confirm details on the company website, log the source URL, score each lead with a transparent model, verify emails only for qualified leads, then QA a 10% sample before delivery.

12. How do you make sure data is accurate?

One source URL per field, controlled values for things like country and size, de-duplication on the domain, and a spot check of 10% of rows. If I can't verify something, I leave it blank or mark it unverified rather than guess.

13. What compliance issues matter in cold outreach?

It depends on the country. In the UK, sole traders are treated as individuals and need consent, while limited companies don't need prior consent but must get an opt-out. Australia needs express or inferred consent. Singapore needs "" in the subject line for unsolicited bulk email. The US needs a physical address and an opt-out. I brief the client and they confirm with their counsel.

14. Two sources give different numbers. What do you do?

I report both with their dates and definitions, explain why they might differ and say which I'd rely on and why. In my market research, market-size estimates ranged from $6.6B to $29.9B because of different definitions.

D. Virtual assistant and operations questions

15. How would you manage my inbox?

I'd do two triage passes a day using five labels: Action-You, Action-Me, Waiting, Read-Later and Receipts. I'd reply only with approved templates and draft anything sensitive for your approval. Each day ends with a three-line note: handled, needs you, waiting.

16. How do you use AI tools with client data?

Only with tools the client approves, never with confidential information otherwise, and always with a human check. For example, AI can draft minutes, but I confirm every owner and due date against my notes.

17. Tell me about a process you documented.

I wrote an email-management SOP covering purpose, frequency, labels, step-by-step rules, what not to send and templates, so a replacement assistant could follow it on day one.

18. How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?

I sort by impact and deadline: P1 is today or blocking someone, P2 is this week, P3 is when there's capacity. If two P1s clash, I ask the client which comes first instead of guessing.

E. Questions to ask the interviewer

  1. What does success look like in the first 30 days?
  2. Which tools and access will I use, and is there a policy for AI tools?
  3. How do you prefer updates: daily notes, Slack or a weekly call?
  4. What is the most common reason past assistants or contractors didn't work out?

F. Test-task checklist

  • Re-read the instructions and list every constraint before starting.
  • Do a small part and check it against the brief.
  • Log sources and assumptions.
  • Submit on time, with a two-line summary and any open questions.
